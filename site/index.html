An agent drafts fixes to an AI floor plan. A drafter approves each one.

PlanTrust checks every room of the AI plan against the 360-degree tour and the technician's notes. Code fixes what a rule can fix. A small agent proposes an edit only where the words in the notes decide. Nothing changes the plan until a drafter approves it.

Why I built this

  • The floor plan drives the estimate. Wall lengths, floor area, ceiling height, doors and which rooms are wet become the quantities that are priced, so a wrong plan means a wrong price.
  • Drafters are the expensive, slow step. Sending every plan to a human throws away the speed; trusting every plan puts wrong quantities into claims.
  • So the useful questions are: where is plain code the right answer, where does a model earn its place, how should its output look so a drafter can check it fast, and how do we know it works before a drafter relies on it?

What this shows, and what it does not

It shows the method on synthetic data: code first, a model only where words decide, a drafter check on every edit, a keyword baseline to beat, guards in code, and numbers written by scripts.

It does not show real accuracy, real drafters (the drafter here is simulated and knows the true house), images (voxtral reads text, not pictures) or production scale.

Next, with real data: run in shadow mode next to the drafters, let drafters approve, auto-apply only where the ladder allows, and turn every drafter correction into a test case.

Three questions I would ask your drafters: which plan mistakes cost you the most; what tolerance on walls and ceilings you accept; and how you count an archway or an opening with no door.

From trusted plan to draft estimate

Part 2 takes a room the plan check left with nothing open and drafts its mitigation line items. The quantities come from the floor plan, the choice of items comes from past jobs and the technician's scope notes, and every item shows its evidence.

In production, prices come from Xactimate through the existing Verisk integration. This demo uses a mock price source with example prices, because real Xactimate price lists are licensed. The agents do not change; only the price source does.

Limits

  • Synthetic houses of rectangular rooms, with errors and notes I generated myself, and simulated tour measurements with simple noise. Real tours and real notes are messier.
  • Illustrative prices for two items (wet drywall removal, drying fans by the IICRC (Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification) rule of thumb). They are not real price lists.
  • A weak, free model: Mistral voxtral-small-latest, the one model with a usable free allowance on this account. It sometimes answers the wrong question or writes a tool call as text, and it does not give the same answer every time. The guards are in code for that reason; a stronger model would likely do better, and the harness, tests and scoring stay the same.
  • The estimation agent over-includes. It finds more of the real items than the frequency rule, but it also adds extra items a human must strike out; see the precision column.
